# Internal Reporting

## Overview

Internal reporting encompasses documents and analyses created for internal stakeholders within an organization. These reports provide critical insights into various aspects of the business, including competitive landscapes, product performance, market trends, and operational efficiency. Their primary purpose is to inform strategic decisions, guide product development, refine marketing strategies, and support sales efforts.

## Key Details

Internal reports often feature detailed [Competitive Analysis](./competitive-analysis.md), [Product Specifications](./product-specifications.md), and actionable recommendations. An example of such a report, a "Competitive landscape Q3 2026" from [Aurora Labs](./aurora-labs.md), illustrates common components:

Reports frequently include detailed comparisons of competitor products, often categorized by market segment.

### Battlecard Updates

Internal reports often include specific recommendations for updating internal sales or marketing [Battlecards](./battlecards.md), which are tools used to equip sales teams with information about competitors.

*   **SenseNode:** Emphasize the subscription cost over a three-year period when comparing against competitors.
*   **TeaBuddy:** Clarify that TeaBuddy is not a direct competitor but rather a potential [Partnership](./partnership.md) for [Co-marketing](./co-marketing.md) initiatives.

### Internal Review and Correction

A key function of internal reporting is to identify and rectify discrepancies in internal or external-facing documentation before publication.

*   A specific contradiction was noted regarding a product's battery type in an "[Amazon](./amazon.md) draft," requiring correction before the draft was published.

### Authorship and Context

Internal reports are typically authored by specific individuals (e.g., [Jonah Park](./jonah-park.md)) and are often tagged with relevant market segments, product names, or strategic themes to aid in organization and retrieval.
