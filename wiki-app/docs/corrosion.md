# Corrosion

## Overview

Corrosion is a natural process that degrades materials, typically metals, through chemical or electrochemical reactions with their environment. In the context of sensor hardware, particularly soil probes, corrosion can significantly impact device longevity and performance, especially in challenging environments like Acidic Soil. Understanding and mitigating corrosion is crucial for the long-term reliability of monitoring equipment.

## Key Details

*   **Impact on Soil Probes**: In acidic soil conditions, inexpensive Capacitive Soil Probes have been observed to fail within 6 to 9 months due to corrosion. This rapid degradation highlights the need for robust material choices or protective measures.
*   **Mitigation Strategies**:
    *   **Coated Probes**: The SenseNode SN-400 utilizes a coated probe design to resist corrosion. Replacement probes for this model are available for approximately $12.
    *   **Gold-Flashed PCBs**: The Aurora beta probe incorporates a gold-flashed Printed Circuit Board (PCB) as a potential corrosion-resistant measure. The long-term effectiveness of this specific approach is currently untested.
*   **Documentation and Cost Considerations**:
    *   The issue of soil probe corrosion and its impact on device lifespan should be thoroughly documented, particularly in hardware-related pages.
    *   When comparing different sensor solutions, the Total Cost of Ownership (TCO) should account for probe durability and potential replacement costs due to corrosion. This is a key factor for long-term deployments.
