# Network Engineering

## Overview

Network engineering encompasses the design, implementation, and management of computer networks. This page synthesizes observations and tasks related to network performance, hardware considerations, power management, and data handling within a development context. Key areas include challenges with [Mesh Networking](./mesh-networking.md) scalability and [Power Consumption](./power-consumption.md), alongside broader discussions on hardware durability and [Data Ownership](./data-ownership.md).

## Related Concepts

*   **Mesh Networking**: A network topology where nodes cooperate to route data, often used in distributed sensor networks or IoT devices. The observed issues highlight challenges in scalability and stability.
*   **Power Consumption**: A critical factor in network device design, especially for battery-powered or remote nodes. The 110µA spike indicates a specific power draw event.
*   **Ingress Protection (IP Ratings)**: Standards defining the sealing effectiveness of electrical enclosures against intrusion from foreign bodies (like dirt and dust) and moisture. Essential for the durability of network hardware in various environments.
*   **Data Ownership**: A concept emphasizing user control over data generated by their devices, particularly relevant for sensor networks and privacy.
*   **System Automation**: The use of scheduled tasks (like cron jobs) for maintenance, monitoring, or data processing within network infrastructure.
*   **Solar Trickle Charging**: A method of slowly charging a battery using solar power, often employed for low-power, remote network devices.
*   **Product Development Phases**: The "beta" phase indicates a stage where a product is released for testing by a limited audience, with known issues potentially tolerated for early feedback.
