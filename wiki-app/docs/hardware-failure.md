# Hardware Failure

## Overview

Hardware failure refers to the malfunction or complete breakdown of physical components within a system. This page specifically details common failure modes observed in capacitive soil probes, particularly corrosion when exposed to acidic soil conditions, and explores different design approaches to mitigate these issues.

## Key Details

*   **Capacitive Soil Probe Corrosion**: A significant hardware failure mode identified for capacitive soil probes is corrosion, particularly when deployed in acidic soil environments.
    *   **Lifespan**: Cheap, uncoated probes typically fail within 6–9 months under acidic soil conditions due to corrosion.
    *   **Mitigation Strategies**:
        *   **Coated Probes**: The SenseNode SN-400 utilizes a coated probe design to enhance durability and resistance to corrosion. Replacement coated probes are available for approximately $12.
        *   **Gold-Flashed PCBs**: The Aurora beta probe incorporates a gold-flashed Printed Circuit Board (PCB) as a potential solution to improve long-term resistance to corrosion. However, its long-term performance in various soil conditions remains untested.
*   **Impact on Products**: This issue affects devices like the Nova Widget and SenseNode, which rely on such probes for environmental monitoring.
*   **Considerations**: The durability and lifespan of probes significantly impact the total cost of ownership for sensor systems.
