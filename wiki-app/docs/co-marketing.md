# Co-marketing

## Overview
Co-marketing refers to a collaborative marketing effort between two or more entities, often involving shared branding or promotional activities. In the context of Trademark Clearance, co-marketing arrangements require specific guidelines to ensure proper brand separation and avoid potential confusion or infringement issues.

## Key Details
*   **Logo Separation Requirement**: For co-marketing initiatives, a strict guideline mandates that participating logos must be separated by a minimum distance of 24 pixels. This ensures clear distinction between the brands involved.
*   **Context**: This guideline emerged from a legal review concerning Trademark Clearance for products like MeshSync and TeaBuddy, indicating the importance of clear branding even when products or services might be related or marketed together.
*   **Entities Involved**: The co-marketing discussions involve entities such as Aurora Labs and TeaBuddy (represented by Alex Kim), particularly in relation to their respective marks like MeshSync and TeaBuddy.

## Related Concepts
*   **Trademark Clearance**: The process of ensuring a brand name, logo, or slogan is legally available for use.
*   **Brand Guidelines**: Rules and standards for the consistent application of a brand's identity, including logo usage.
*   **Logo Separation**: A design principle and legal requirement to maintain distinct visual boundaries between different brand logos, especially in co-branded materials.
*   **Crowded Mark**: A trademark that is difficult to protect or register due to many similar existing marks in the marketplace (e.g., "Puck" alone).
*   **Product Classifications (e.g., Class 21)**: International classifications used to categorize goods and services for trademark registration purposes.
